Two market-size estimates are circulating and they measure different things: the narrower $27.5B→$48.8B figure covers weight-loss-labeled GLP-1 drugs only, while the broader $68.7B→$195B figure includes the (larger) existing type-2-diabetes GLP-1 base. Either way, this remains a duopoly-with-challengers market: Lilly and Novo Nordisk control the overwhelming majority of current revenue, but the entire industry is mid-transition from weekly injectables to oral pills and less-frequent dosing, which is exactly where the next competitive battle — and the next M&A wave — is being fought.

Porter's Five Forces

Threat of new entrants — a crowded clinical pipeline (Viking, Structure, Amgen, Zealand, Boehringer, Roche/Carmot) but Phase 3-scale trials and peptide/biologic manufacturing scale are high barriers to organic entry; large pharma increasingly enters via acquisition rather than in-house development.
MODERATE
Buyer power — rising: large employers (PepsiCo and others) are dropping GLP-1 coverage to control costs, PBMs are pushing formulary battles, and TrumpRx-style direct-to-consumer pricing is compressing net prices — a real new headwind versus a year ago.
RISING
Supplier power (peptide/biologic manufacturing capacity) — easing: tirzepatide and semaglutide came off the FDA drug-shortage list in Dec 2024/Feb 2025 as both majors built out capacity; no longer the acute constraint it was in 2023–2024.
MODERATE
Threat of substitutes — compounded/generic-copy GLP-1s were a major substitute channel; the FDA's 2026 crackdown (proposed permanent 503B Bulks List exclusion, 55+ warning letters) is actively closing that door, a tailwind for branded incumbents. Bariatric surgery and lifestyle apps remain minor substitutes.
CLOSING
Rivalry among incumbents — intense and escalating: Novo's false-advertising lawsuit against Lilly, realized price cuts (-9% for Lilly in Q2), and an active "metabolic land grab" M&A race (Pfizer, Merck, Amgen, Novo all reportedly circling third-tier biotechs) for the next-generation asset.
HIGH

Net read: the FDA closing the compounding side-door reinforces the branded duopoly's moat structurally, but rising buyer pushback (employer coverage drops) and intra-duopoly price/legal warfare are compressing margins and growth expectations faster than the market previously priced in — a plausible real explanation for LLY's pullback from its all-time high, distinct from simple profit-taking.

Technology disruption

The category is mid-transition on two axes simultaneously. Delivery format: oral small-molecule pills (Lilly's Foundayo/orforglipron, Novo's oral semaglutide, Structure's aleniglipron) are displacing weekly injectables, unlocking primary-care distribution and adherence at a scale injectables cannot match. Dosing frequency: Amgen's MariTide, a peptide-antibody conjugate, enables monthly dosing versus weekly injections — directly targeting the real-world discontinuation problem that limits weekly GLP-1 regimens. Further out, triple agonists and amylin-pathway drugs (Novo's amycretin/zenagamtide) are chasing an efficacy tier beyond today's dual agonists, and indication expansion into MASH/NASH, obstructive sleep apnea, and cardiovascular disease is already in Phase 3 programs — a source of TAM expansion not fully captured in current weight-loss-market sizing.

Regulatory environment

Tailwinds: the FDA's April 2026 proposal to permanently exclude semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B Bulks List would close the legal pathway for mass compounding; 55+ warning letters issued to telehealth marketers in March and June 2026 are already suppressing the compounded-copy channel.
Risks: the TrumpRx direct-to-consumer pricing initiative and broader most-favored-nation pricing pressure are pushing net prices down industry-wide; the FTC has signaled increased scrutiny of "mega-merger" obesity M&A, meaning a Pfizer/Viking-type deal could face real antitrust friction or forced divestitures; Medicare drug-price-negotiation exposure is a growing overhang as CMS's negotiation program expands its reach into diabetes/obesity GLP-1s over time.

Emerging Opportunities

  • Underserved niche — scarcity-premium biotech M&A targets: with only two incumbents controlling ~87% of revenue, differentiated third-tier assets (Viking's dual sub-Q+oral platform, Structure's oral small molecule) carry real takeout optionality that a two-company market structure makes unusually concentrated.
  • Technology adoption gap — dosing frequency: monthly-dosing candidates (Amgen's MariTide) directly address the adherence/discontinuation problem that limits today's weekly-injectable standard of care — an underexploited differentiator relative to how much attention oral pills are getting.
  • Regulatory tailwind — compounding channel closure: the FDA's 2026 enforcement wave should structurally redirect volume from compounded copies back to branded drugs over 2026–2027, a real (if hard to size precisely) demand tailwind for LLY and NVO specifically.
  • Consolidation targets: Viking Therapeutics (38.5% Polymarket-implied buyout odds) and Structure Therapeutics (positioned to inherit the scarcity premium if Viking is taken first) are both named repeatedly across sources as live M&A targets for Pfizer, Merck, or Amgen.
  • Indication-expansion optionality: MASH/NASH, sleep apnea, and cardiovascular-disease programs already underway (Amgen's MARITIME program spans several of these) could expand the addressable market well beyond current weight-loss-only sizing — genuine upside optionality embedded in existing large-cap pipelines that isn't yet a specific, actionable name.
