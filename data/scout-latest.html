Market landscape

Demand trajectory
  • Global data-center power demand projected +220% vs 2023 → ~1,350 TWh by 2030 (Goldman Sachs, Apr-2026).
  • US DC capacity +197% 2025-2030 → ~95 GW; DC share of US electricity ~6% → ~11% by 2030.
  • Hyperscaler AI capex raised to ~$750B in 2026, crossing $1T in 2027.
  • Grid investment ~$550B in 2026, with multi-year backlogs in transformers, switchgear and turbines.
Porter's Five Forces — quick read
  • Rivalry: moderate — demand outruns supply, so backlogs, not price wars, set the pace.
  • New entrants: low — capital intensity, certification, multi-year lead times and installed base are steep barriers.
  • Supplier power: elevated — transformer/turbine and skilled-labor scarcity favor incumbents with capacity.
  • Buyer power: low-moderate — hyperscalers are big but supply-constrained and locking in via long PPAs.
  • Substitutes: low — electrons have no substitute; competition is among generation/equipment forms.

The most promising lead — Vertiv (VRT)

Of the four, Vertiv is the tightest fit to the AI-power chokepoint. It is not a diversified industrial with a data-center segment — it is the data-center power-and-thermal business, and its power/cooling is designed into Nvidia's GB-class rack reference architectures, giving it design-win lock-in as racks scale in density. A ~$15B backlog built on 252% Q1-2026 order growth and a $13.5-14.0B revenue guide (~30% YoY) put it at the top of the group for growth.

Crucially, the entry is favorable: despite +62% YTD, VRT trades ~31% below its 52-week high and near its 13-week low, a meaningful pullback in an otherwise-extended theme. It is the highest-conviction, largest-sized suggestion here — best staged in rather than bought in one clip given the volatility.

Risks & emerging threats

  • Theme concentration / AI-capex beta. All four rise and fall with hyperscaler capex, which also drives the account's NVDA/MU/SNDK. Sizing the whole sleeve to ~4-5% of NAV keeps it a satellite, not a doubling-down.
  • Extended valuations & momentum. VRT/PWR/GEV are +45-62% YTD; the pullbacks off highs are the reason to stage entries rather than chase.
  • Capex-cycle risk. Any hyperscaler capex pause would hit order growth first — watch quarterly bookings/backlog, not just revenue.
  • Idiosyncratic (CEG). Nuclear PPA pricing and regulatory/relicensing risk explain its -22% YTD; sized smallest for that reason.
  • Supply-chain normalization. Today's pricing power rests on transformer/turbine scarcity; easing lead times would compress margins for equipment makers.
